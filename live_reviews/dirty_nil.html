The Dirty Nil

The Key Club, Leeds 07/07/2026

I first heard of The Dirty Nil a few months ago when Guitar World ran a feature on their new album. In the article, guitarist/vocalist Luke Bentham extolled the virtues of a simple rig - Les Paul into Marshall stack. Call me old school, but this is the sort of thing that makes me pay attention to a band these days. I've never had much time for digital modelling stuff, which bands increasingly swear by both live and in the studio in the 2020s. If a "guitar amp" is full of ICs, circuit boards and digital readouts rather than the lovely glowing things we call valves (or tubes if you're on the other side of the Atlantic), it's just not cool. Therefore, even before I'd heard a note of their music, I had my suspicions that The Dirty Nil were pretty cool.

It turns out I was right. Their latest album, The Lash, is an exciting mix of punk, metal and good old hard rock delivered via some ripping guitar tones and pummeling drums. No over-produced crap here, just straight up killer riffs and songs packed with hooks. It ended up being quite a good time for getting into the band as not long after the Guitar World article, they embarked on a fairly extensive UK tour, which is what has brought them to Yorkshire tonight, at one of the best small venues in God's Own Country: The Key Club.


First off, very honorable mentions must also go to tonight's support bands. Smug LLC opens the night - think a one man Canadian version of Sleaford Mods, with each track accompanied by a lyric video playing on a big screen on stage. It sounds a bit out there, but it totally works live. Second up are Manchester four piece Bruise Control. With a sound that lands somewhere between Fugazi and Pissed Jeans, they're full of hardcore energy and are the perfect pre-cursor to The Dirty Nil.

Which brings us to the headliners. There's definitely no let up in the energy with opener Celebration's massive pinch-harmonic drenched opening riff, and this continues through Rock N' Roll Band and Gallop Of The Hounds, both from latest album The Lash. Tonight's setlist is split fairly evenly over The Nil's five studio albums, demonstrating just how consistent they've been over the years. One of the main things that hits you when you see the band live is how much noise they make for a three piece. There's always a risk in a one guitar band of a song's power suffering when the guitarist takes a solo, but this isn't a problem here, with the lack of mid-range filled in by a massive bass presence.
Although they don't outwardly claim it, there's plenty of metal in The Dirty Nil - from the artwork for The Lash, which has a definite feel of "Norweigian black metal demo tape" about it, to the songs themselves (not least the aforementioned Celebration). Make no mistake, this is a heavy band. Their metal credentials are further enhanced by bookending live favourite Doom Boy with the immortal opening riffs from Slayer's Raining Blood. After a tight 75 minute-ish set, the final song - 2023's Nicer Guy - is introduced with words to live by "Be good to ourselves, be good to each other, and fuck AI". I couldn't agree more.

Setlist: Celebration / No Weaknesses / Rock N' Roll Band / Gallop Of The Hounds / Pain of Infinity / Fail in Time / Bathed in Light / That Don't Mean It Won't Sting / That's What Heaven Feels Like / Hang Yer Moon / Damage Control / Wrestle Yü to Hüsker Dü / Smoking Is Magic / Friends in the Sky / Free Rein to Passions / Blunt Force Concussion / Doom Boy / Evil Side / Nicer Guy
